# Leap: reach a block by the words you can see

Status: **approved 2026-08-16.** Implemented as ticket H5.

Supersedes the block-labelling leap shipped in H3
([2026-08-16-chat-navigation.md](../exec-plans/active/2026-08-16-chat-navigation.md)).

## Problem

`s` today paints a label on every visible *block*. That works, but it asks the
reader to translate: they are looking at a sentence, and to reach it they must
find the label attached to the box the sentence lives in. On a long agent
message the label sits at the top of a screenful of prose, nowhere near the
thing being aimed at.

leap.nvim solved this years ago. You do not label containers; you label
occurrences of what the reader is already reading. The reader types the two
characters they can see, and the label appears on the text itself.

## Desired outcome

`s`, then two characters, then one label character, and the block holding that
occurrence is focused. Three keystrokes to anywhere on screen, aimed by reading
rather than by looking up.

## In scope

- Text search across the focused column's visible text.
- Match painting and label chips.
- Label assignment, ordering, and paging.
- The mode's key ownership, and every way out of it.

## Out of scope

- A text cursor. There is none, and this does not introduce one — see the first
  settled decision.
- Searching unfocused columns, the composer, or any chrome.
- Backward search (`S`). There is no cursor for "backward" to be relative to.
- Operating on the matched text: `a` and `copy` continue to act on the block.

## Settled decisions

1. **A leap lands on a block, not on a character.** The match is how the reader
   aims; the block containing it is what gets focused, exactly as `j` and `k`
   focus it. Nothing flashes and nothing scrolls to the match itself. This
   keeps one focus model in the app rather than two.
2. **Every match is labelled, including several in one block.** Searching `is`
   against "This is" labels both occurrences. They lead to the same block, and
   that is fine: the reader aimed at a word, not at a container, and having to
   work out which occurrences "count" would be worse than a redundant label.
3. **Only the focused column is searched.** Everything else the keyboard does
   acts on the focused column alone; `s` must not be the one exception. To
   leap into the next thread, press `l` first.
4. **Only visible text is searched** — what is inside the column's scroll
   viewport right now. A collapsed tool body holds no visible text, so it holds
   no matches. This is what makes the promise honest: a hint means the
   destination is in front of you.
5. **The whole transcript dims while leaping.** Every block goes to the
   unfocused treatment — 50% opacity, no colour — and the matches are painted
   over it. The reader is choosing between matches, so the matches should be
   the only lit thing on screen.
6. **Smartcase.** All-lowercase input matches any case; one uppercase character
   makes the whole pattern exact. The convention every editor already taught.
7. **Labels are assigned nearest-first**, measured from the focused block, or
   from the top of the viewport when nothing is focused. The closest match gets
   the easiest key. Order: `sfnjklhodweimbuyvrgtaqpcxz`, leap.nvim's own set,
   which is chosen for home-row reach.
8. **More matches than labels are paged.** `space` shows the next group,
   `backspace` the previous. Labels are reused per group rather than growing to
   two characters: a leap is three keystrokes, and a fourth defeats it.
9. **Backspace before the labels appear deletes the last typed character.**
   After they appear it pages. The mode is in two phases and the key means the
   obvious thing in each.
10. **A pattern that matches nothing ends the mode**, leaving the focus where
    it was. Guessing is worse than doing nothing.
11. **`esc` ends the mode** at any phase, leaving the focus where it was.
12. **The mode owns every key while it is up.** That is what lets a label be
    `j`, or a searched character be `a`, without colliding with the binding.
    It ranks below the confirm modal, the commit card and the block menu, and
    above ordinary navigation — the rank the current hint mode already has.

## Deliberate deviations from leap.nvim

Both are stated so a reader of this file knows they were chosen, not missed.

- **Autojump only on a single match.** leap.nvim jumps to the nearest match as
  soon as the second character is typed, while leaving the other labels live,
  and relies on a "safe labels" set that its users never press straight after a
  motion. This app has no such set: `j`, `k`, `a`, `l` and `h` are all live the
  instant a leap ends, and any of them could be a leftover label. So the mode
  ends the moment it jumps, and it only jumps without a label when there is
  exactly one match.
- **No *labels* after the first character, but the matches are painted.**
  leap.nvim shows labels once one character is typed. A window of code has few
  single-character matches; a column of English prose has hundreds, and a
  label on each is noise rather than a preview. So the first character paints
  every occurrence of it and nothing more: the reader sees at once whether
  they are aiming somewhere dense or somewhere rare, and picks their second
  character accordingly. Labels arrive with the second character.

## Acceptance criteria

1. In a focused thread column, `s` dims every block and waits. Nothing is
   painted yet.
2. Typing one character paints every occurrence of it in the visible text, with
   no labels.
3. Typing the second character narrows the paint to occurrences of the pair and
   puts a label chip on each.
4. Typing a label focuses the block holding that occurrence, ends the mode, and
   restores the normal dim — the leapt-to block bright, the rest dim.
5. Searching a pattern that occurs twice in one message labels both, and either
   label focuses that message.
6. A pattern with exactly one match focuses its block immediately, with no
   label typed.
7. A pattern with no match ends the mode and moves nothing.
8. `esc` at any phase ends the mode and moves nothing.
9. With more matches than labels, `space` and `backspace` cycle groups and the
   chips change accordingly.
10. `s` in a terminal column does nothing.
11. `Sync` matches only `Sync`; `sync` matches `Sync` and `sync` both.
12. Text scrolled out of view is never a destination.

## Implementation constraints

- **Paint with the CSS Custom Highlight API** (`Highlight`, `CSS.highlights`),
  which is available in this Electron's Chromium. It paints ranges without
  inserting elements, so Svelte's rendered DOM is untouched and no keyed list
  is disturbed mid-stream. Wrapping matches in `<span>`s would fight the
  renderer for ownership of the same nodes.
- **Label chips live in one overlay layer**, a single absolutely-positioned
  child of the column's scroll box, positioned from each range's client rect.
  They must not be rendered inside blocks: blocks carry paint containment from
  `content-visibility: auto`, which has already clipped one absolutely
  positioned surface in this feature and would clip these too.
- Ranges are found by walking the column's text nodes. The walk skips nothing
  by tag — a match inside a tool body's code line is as good a destination as
  one in a message.
- A range maps back to a block by walking up to the nearest element carrying a
  registered nav id, which the existing block registry already knows.
- The search and the label assignment are pure functions over
  `{ text, navId }[]`, unit-testable without a DOM. Only the walk and the paint
  touch the browser.

## Validation

- Unit: pattern matching with smartcase, label assignment and ordering, paging,
  the single-match case, the no-match case.
- Through the real key path: `s` then two characters then a label moves the
  focus; `esc` at each phase moves nothing.
- In the running app: both occurrences of a repeated pattern are labelled and
  both work; only visible text is matched; a collapsed tool body yields no
  matches; the chips are not clipped at a column edge.

## Risks

- **Chip placement at the column edges.** A match at the far right of a line
  leaves no room for its chip. Needs a rule — most likely the chip sits over
  the character *after* the match, as leap.nvim's does, and shifts left when
  there is no room.
- **Re-measuring while a turn streams.** Tokens arriving reflow the text under
  a painted match. The mode is short-lived, but a streaming column can move
  under it. Simplest answer is to re-run the walk on the column's scroll and
  resize, and accept that a mid-leap reflow may shift a chip by a line.

## Known limitation

A pattern is matched **within one text node**. A pair straddling the boundary
between, say, prose and an inline code span is not found. This costs a
destination, never a wrong one, and the alternative — concatenating each
block's text and mapping offsets back through a node table — is a large amount
of machinery for a case that arises at emphasis boundaries. Revisit if it is
ever noticed in use.

## Open question

**Should `s` be reachable from NORMAL as well as READ?** It is today, and it
enters READ. Keeping that means `s` is a way *into* the transcript, not only a
way around it. No reason found to change it; noted because it makes `s` the
only binding that both enters a mode and does work in it.
